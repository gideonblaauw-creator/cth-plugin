## 1. Why trust cannot stay behind closed doors

Security built behind closed doors protects only the few who own the models. In an agentic, tokenized economy, software does not just display information. It moves money, signs agreements, and reports impact on our behalf. When the stack is opaque, trust becomes a brand promise from a vendor, not something anyone can test.

I believe the next decade of finance in the Americas will run on agents and tokenized assets. If those systems cannot be inspected, adapted, and challenged by the institutions and communities they serve, we do not have security. We have dependence.

## 2. Where I come from

I learned this first in climate and impact data, not in a cybersecurity lab.

Closed, unverifiable numbers breed greenwashing. Open, verifiable data unlocks capital. That pattern shaped how I work as Co-Founder of CleantechHUB Latin America since 2019. Our work includes **Ruta Verde**, **Nexus**, and **Origo**: infrastructure that helps green finance find trustworthy impact signals across Latin America.

I watched funds hesitate not because the opportunity was weak, but because they could not trace a claim to evidence. I watched local innovators lose deals because their data lived in someone else's silo. Openness was not ideology. It was the difference between capital that flows and capital that stalls.

That experience is the lens I bring to AI security. Models are the new data layer. Agents are the new intermediaries. If we repeat the closed-data mistakes of the last green-finance cycle, we will build a faster machine for the same distrust.

## 3. The shift underway

Three forces are converging.

**Agentic finance.** AI agents already draft, reconcile, and route decisions. They will increasingly move money, negotiate terms, and file regulatory reports. Each action needs a clear line of accountability to a human who can answer for it.

**Tokenized assets.** Real-world assets and impact claims are moving on-chain or through tokenized wrappers. Value follows the token; risk follows the data behind the token.

**Latin America at the center.** Bay Area innovation and LatAm enterprise and finance are connecting faster than either side can audit the other's stack. Americas Innovation Council (AIC), a Run Up Holdings project where Frank connects Bay Area AI with LatAm finance and enterprise, sits in that bridge. I serve as Associate Founder at AIC. Our question is not whether AI arrives in regional finance. It is whether LatAm institutions can inspect what they are asked to trust.

If LatAm finance runs on systems it cannot see inside, sovereignty is nominal. Compliance becomes checkbox theater. Impact becomes marketing.

## 4. Why openness is the security model

The open-source software movement did not win because secrecy was immoral. It won because many eyes on the code found flaws faster than any single vendor could, and because organizations could patch, fork, and host on their own terms.

AI needs the same logic. **Open models and open weights** allow independent auditors, regulators, universities, and local innovators to test behavior, probe failure modes, and adapt deployments to local law and language. Security comes from **scrutiny**, not from a proprietary black box and a terms-of-service clause.

Closed models may be fine for narrow experiments. They are a poor foundation for public infrastructure: pension rails, rural banking, climate disclosure, or tokenized green bonds. When the model is secret, the institution cannot prove what the agent did. When the weights are locked, the community cannot harden the system after an incident.

Openness also advances justice. If only the largest banks and hyperscalers can afford frontier models, small institutions and the Global South get priced out of safe AI. That is not a market outcome. It is a security gap waiting to become a headline.

## 5. Principles

These are the commitments I ask builders, institutions, and investors to treat as defaults, not exceptions.

1. **Open models and open weights by default** for infrastructure that touches finance, impact, or public data. Proprietary stacks must justify why openness is impossible, not the other way around.

2. **Security through scrutiny, not secrecy.** Publish what can be tested. Document data flows. Welcome third-party audit.

3. **Data sovereignty for LatAm institutions and communities.** Training data, inference, and logs should respect where data lives and who governs it.

4. **Verifiable agents.** Every material agent action must be traceable to an accountable human, with tamper-evident logs and clear escalation paths.

5. **Tokenized assets backed by auditable data.** No token should outrun the evidence chain behind it. Impact and climate claims require the same rigor as financial statements.

6. **Climate and impact data as critical infrastructure.** Treat disclosure pipelines like power grids: resilient, inspectable, and shared where openness reduces systemic risk.

7. **Fair access.** Pricing and licensing must not lock out credit unions, municipal funds, startups, and public universities from participating in a secure AI stack.

8. **Humans stay accountable.** Agents assist; they do not inherit liability. Governance names who answers when something breaks.

## 7. What I ask

**Builders:** Release open weights for models destined for finance and impact workflows, or publish reproducible evaluation suites that institutions can run themselves. Ship agent frameworks with audit hooks, not only demos.

**Banks and funds:** Demand auditability in procurement. Ask where weights live, what data trained the model, and how agent actions are logged. Treat opaque stacks as contingent risk, not competitive advantage.

**Regulators:** Reward openness. Sandboxes, procurement guidance, and disclosure rules should make verifiable AI cheaper than black-box AI. Align tokenized asset rules with auditable underlying data.

**Investors:** Fund open infrastructure: eval harnesses, sovereign hosting, climate data commons, and agent accountability tooling. The moat is trust at scale, not a single closed model.

## 8. The vision I attest to

I want a financial and impact system where agentic software and tokenized assets accelerate inclusion and climate solutions because **anyone can verify the stack**, not because everyone must trust a logo.

An open, agentic, tokenized world can be secure, not in spite of transparency, but because of it. Scrutiny is the immune system. Sovereignty is the policy. Accountability is the human line we do not automate away.

If you share that vision, say so plainly. Build for inspection. Buy for auditability. Regulate for evidence.
