# CleantechHUB Grant Lifecycle Skill

End-to-end playbook for evaluating, scoping, drafting, budgeting, and submitting grant applications and competitive bids — built from 4+ years of real CTH grant history (2022–2026), encoding every pattern that made applications succeed and every mistake that caused them to fail. Includes lessons from BID/IDB competitive procurement processes.

**Reference data**: The full grant history lives in the Grants project folder at `CTH_Grant_History_2022-2026.md`. Read it when you need specific dates, amounts, or funder details beyond what's encoded here.

## 1. Opportunity Evaluation — The GO/NO-GO Gate

Before investing any drafting time, every grant opportunity must pass through a structured evaluation. CTH operates with limited capacity (small team, multiple concurrent programs), so saying no to the wrong grants is as important as saying yes to the right ones.

### 1a. The Fit Scorecard

Score each opportunity 1–5 on these nine criteria. These are the actual dimensions institutional funders evaluate, weighted by how often they've determined outcomes in CTH's history:

| # | Criterion | Weight | What to assess |
|---|-----------|--------|----------------|
| 1 | **Climate / SDG alignment** | Critical | Does the grant's mandate match CTH's core mission (cleantech ecosystem building, climate adaptation, startup acceleration in LatAm)? CTH scores 5/5 here consistently — if alignment is below 4, stop. |
| 2 | **Partnership structure** | Critical | Does the funder require a consortium? Does CTH have a responsive, committed partner? P4G R5 stalled partly because of document issues between partners. Energy Catalyst R11 died because Carbon Trust never responded to 3 follow-ups. Rule: if the required lead partner hasn't confirmed in writing within 2 weeks of first outreach, downgrade to 1 and flag as high-risk. |
| 3 | **Track record / references** | High | Can CTH demonstrate relevant past performance? Strongest when applying to funders where CTH has delivered before (P4G R4 won because R3 was executed well; UNDP-AFCIA R2 won because R1 reporting was clean). For new funders, the Briter Bridges report + P4G closeout + UNDP terminal evaluation are the three strongest references. |
| 4 | **Co-financing / cost share** | High | Can CTH demonstrate co-financing? This has historically been a weak spot (3/5). Co-financing often comes from in-kind contributions. If the funder requires >20% cash co-financing, flag as a constraint and identify sources early. |
| 5 | **Impact measurement** | High | Does CTH have the data the funder needs? This is CTH's single biggest gap (2/5). DGGF follow-up explicitly exposed this. If the funder requires a rigorous M&E framework with baseline data, CTH needs to either build one before applying or partner with someone who has one. |
| 6 | **Budget discipline** | High | Can CTH structure a budget that keeps salaries under 40% of total? P4G R5 was rejected specifically because salaries hit 60%. Every budget must be reviewed against this threshold before submission. |
| 7 | **Innovation / scalability** | Medium | Does the proposal showcase CTH's innovative models? AI Logic Center, REIN Hubs franchise, Sustenttia platform are strong differentiators. Score higher when the funder values novel approaches (Climate KIC, Energy Catalyst) vs. proven delivery (UNDP, P4G). |
| 8 | **Local ownership / presence** | Medium | CTH is Bogotá-based with team across Colombia, Peru, Guatemala, El Salvador, Costa Rica. This is a 5/5 strength. Emphasize it for funders that prioritize local presence (P4G, UNDP, Expertise France). |
| 9 | **Financial governance** | Medium | External accounting via Soluciones JV, dedicated bank account (Banco de Occidente for UNDP). But late salary payments and cash flow gaps between grants are a known vulnerability. If the funder requires audited financials, verify CTH has current ones. |

### 1b. GO/NO-GO Decision Framework

**Automatic GO** (score ≥ 35/45 and no criterion below 3):
- Funder alignment is clear, partner is confirmed, CTH has track record with this funder or similar.
- Proceed to scoping.

**Conditional GO** (score 25–34 or one criterion at 2):
- Present the scorecard to Gideon with the weak areas highlighted.
- Ask: "Do we have a mitigation plan for [weakness]? Is the potential value worth the drafting investment given current capacity?"
- Wait for explicit approval before proceeding.

**NO-GO** (score < 25 or any criterion at 1):
- Document why in the grant pipeline tracker and move on.
- Past examples of correct NO-GOs: AFAS Foundation (not ANBI-registered), Digital Innovators for Nature (no follow-up capacity), Global Resilience Partnership (too far from CTH core).

### 1c. Capacity Check

Before greenlighting any application, verify:
- **Deadline vs. current load**: If CTH has 2+ other active deadlines within the same 3-week window, the quality will suffer. CTH's lean team means multiple simultaneous proposals degrade all of them.
- **Partner responsiveness**: Has every named consortium partner responded to at least one email in the last 7 days? If not, flag and escalate.
- **Grant-ready package**: Are corporate docs, financials, CVs, and past project summaries current? (See Section 7.)

## 2. Scoping — Defining What CTH Will Actually Do

Scoping happens after the GO decision but before any narrative drafting. The goal is to define a clear boundary around what CTH commits to deliver, so the proposal doesn't overpromise.

### 2a. Scope Definition Template

For every grant, define:

```
PROJECT SCOPE BRIEF
====================
Grant/Program:    [name]
Funder:           [organization]
Period:           [start – end]
Total budget:     [amount + currency]
CTH role:         [lead applicant / co-applicant / implementing partner / subcontractor]
Geographic focus: [countries / regions]

WHAT'S IN SCOPE:
- [Concrete activity 1 — e.g., "Source and screen 50 climate startups in Colombia and Peru"]
- [Concrete activity 2 — e.g., "Deliver 3-month acceleration program with mentoring and demo day"]
- [Concrete activity 3 — e.g., "Produce quarterly M&E reports with KPI dashboard"]

WHAT'S OUT OF SCOPE:
- [Explicit exclusion 1 — e.g., "Direct investment in startups (CTH facilitates, does not invest)"]
- [Explicit exclusion 2 — e.g., "Hardware procurement or physical infrastructure"]

DEPENDENCIES:
- [What must be true for CTH to deliver — e.g., "Partner X provides local office space"]
- [External factor — e.g., "Government permits for pilot site"]
```

### 2b. Scoping Rules from CTH Experience

**Keep the scope proportional to the grant size.** A EUR 30K grant (like CLP26 per country) should have 3–5 activities. A EUR 200K+ grant (like DGGF or P4G) can have 8–12 activities across work packages. Overscoping relative to budget is how CTH ends up with salary costs at 60% — because the activities are too numerous for a small team to deliver without burning hours.

**Name the activities you're good at.** CTH's proven delivery areas (from successful grants): startup sourcing/screening, bootcamp/acceleration delivery, mentoring coordination, demo day/investor matchmaking, ecosystem mapping, grant management/reporting. These should form the core of every scope.

**Exclude what creates liability.** CTH is an ecosystem builder, not an investor or policy implementer. Every scope should explicitly exclude: direct equity investment, policy lobbying, hardware/infrastructure procurement, and anything requiring certifications CTH doesn't hold.

## 4. Drafting — Writing the Grant Narrative

### 4a. Before You Write a Single Word

1. **Read the call/RFP completely.** Extract every requirement, eligibility criterion, and evaluation weight. Make a checklist.
2. **Check the grant-ready package** (Section 7) — are all supporting docs current?
3. **Confirm the scope** (Section 2) is agreed with Gideon and any partners.
4. **Map CTH's track record** to the funder's priorities — which past grants to cite, which references to name.

### 4b. Narrative Structure (Universal Template)

Most institutional grant narratives follow this structure. Adapt section names to match the funder's template, but ensure all content areas are covered:

```
1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (1 page max)
   - Who is CTH, what are we proposing, for how much, over what period
   - One sentence on why CTH is uniquely positioned
   - Expected headline outcome (e.g., "50 climate startups accelerated,
     $2M follow-on funding facilitated")

2. CONTEXT & PROBLEM STATEMENT (1–2 pages)
   - The ecosystem gap in LatAm cleantech
   - Why it matters now (policy window, climate urgency, market readiness)
   - Localize with country-specific data for the target geography

3. PROPOSED APPROACH / METHODOLOGY (2–4 pages)
   - Theory of change (adapted per Section 3b)
   - Activities and workplan (from Scope, Section 2)
   - Partnership model — who does what
   - Innovation — what's new or different about CTH's approach

4. EXPECTED RESULTS & IMPACT (1–2 pages)
   - Outputs, outcomes, impact (from ToC, Section 3a)
   - KPIs with targets and measurement methodology
   - Sustainability — what continues after grant funding ends

5. ORGANIZATIONAL CAPACITY (1 page)
   - CTH background, team, governance
   - Past grant performance (cite P4G, UNDP-AFCIA, CLP track record)
   - References (Briter Bridges, funder testimonials)

6. BUDGET SUMMARY (reference to detailed budget annex)
   - Top-line figures by work package
   - Co-financing summary

7. ANNEXES
   - Detailed budget (see Section 5)
   - Team CVs
   - Letters of support from partners
   - Corporate registration documents
   - Financial statements
```

### 4c. Writing Rules (Learned from 4 Years of Applications)

**Do:**
- Use the funder's own language. If the call says "locally-led adaptation", use that exact phrase in your narrative. Mirror their priorities back to them.
- Quantify everything. "We will accelerate startups" is weak. "We will source 200 applications, screen 50 for bootcamp, accelerate 15 through a 3-month program, and prepare 5 for investor readiness" is strong.
- Show the pipeline, not just activities. Funders want to see the journey: how does a startup enter → progress → exit with measurable outcomes?
- Reference past performance with specific data. "In UNDP-AFCIA R2, CTH supported X startups and filed all reports on time" is more persuasive than "CTH has experience in this area."
- Include a sustainability section that's honest. "The REIN Hubs model generates fee revenue that reduces grant dependency over time" is credible. Don't claim full self-sustainability within one grant cycle unless the numbers back it up.

**Don't:**
- Submit a budget where salaries exceed 40% of total. This was the single biggest factor in P4G R5's rejection. Restructure line items to show programmatic costs (events, mentoring, tools, travel, startup grants) prominently.
- Attach the wrong version of any document. P4G R5 was submitted with an incorrect W&B file. Implement a final checklist (see Section 4d).
- Depend on a partner who hasn't confirmed. If a consortium member hasn't signed a letter of intent or responded to 2 emails, replace them or drop the partnership component.
- Overpromise on impact measurement. If CTH doesn't currently track CO2 reduction at portfolio level, don't claim it as an existing capability. Propose building it.
- Rush a submission. If the deadline is <2 weeks away and the scope isn't agreed, it's better to skip than to submit a weak application that damages CTH's reputation with the funder.

### 4d. Pre-Submission Checklist

Run this checklist before every submission. Every item must be YES or explicitly waived by Gideon.

```
PRE-SUBMISSION CHECKLIST
========================
□ All narrative sections address every requirement in the call
□ Budget total matches the narrative's stated amount
□ Salary line items are ≤40% of total budget
□ Co-financing sources are named and (ideally) confirmed in writing
□ All annexes are the correct, current versions
□ Partner letters of support are signed and dated
□ Team CVs are current (updated within last 6 months)
□ Financial statements are the most recent available
□ Corporate registration docs are valid (not expired)
□ Impact metrics cited in narrative match the M&E framework
□ Submission format matches funder requirements (PDF, online portal, email)
□ Submission deadline allows 24h buffer for technical issues
□ Gideon has reviewed and approved the final package
```

## 5. Budgeting — The Make-or-Break Document

Budget composition has been the single most consequential factor in CTH's grant outcomes. P4G R5's rejection was directly attributed to salary costs at 60% of total.

### 5a. The 40% Rule

**Salary and personnel costs must never exceed 40% of the total grant budget.** This is CTH's hard-learned ceiling. When personnel costs are higher than programmatic costs, funders read it as "this organization is funding itself, not delivering a program."

How to restructure when salaries are running high:
- **Break out programmatic staff time** as activity costs, not salary. If a team member spends 40% of their time delivering bootcamps, that 40% goes under "Program Delivery", not "Personnel."
- **Budget for external expertise** — consultants, mentors, evaluators. These are programmatic, not overhead.
- **Include startup-facing costs** — grants-to-startups, travel stipends, event costs, digital tools, platform subscriptions. These show money flowing to beneficiaries.
- **Co-financing can absorb personnel** — if CTH is contributing staff time as co-financing, show it in the co-finance column, not the grant budget.

### 5c. Budget Red Flags to Catch Before Submission

| Red flag | Why it matters | Fix |
|----------|---------------|-----|
| Personnel > 40% of grant | Triggered P4G R5 rejection | Restructure per 5a |
| No M&E budget line | Signals impact measurement isn't planned | Add 5-8% for M&E |
| Zero travel budget | Unrealistic for multi-country programs | Budget realistically |
| Co-financing unconfirmed | Funder may verify; embarrassing if sources don't confirm | Get written confirmations |
| Overhead > 15% | Most funders cap at 7-12% | Check funder's policy |
| Budget doesn't match narrative | Narrative says "5 countries" but budget only funds travel to 3 | Cross-reference before submission |
| Rounding errors or wrong totals | Creates impression of carelessness | Use Excel formulas, verify totals |

### 5d. Funder-Specific Budget Rules

| Funder | Known requirements |
|--------|-------------------|
| P4G | Strict operational cost threshold. Cost share documentation required. Budget template provided — use theirs exactly |
| UNDP / Adaptation Fund | FSR (Financial Status Report) format. Transaction lists required. Separate bank account preferred |
| Climate KIC / CLP | Simple event-based budgets. Bootcamp costs, travel, prizes. Lower documentation burden |
| DGGF / Triple Jump | Investment fund logic — they look at financial sustainability, not just project budgets |
| GIZ | Detailed line-item budgets with unit costs. German procurement standards |
| Expertise France | Consortium budget with clear partner splits. EU procurement rules apply |
| BID/IDB (CP) | Lowest evaluated price wins. Deliverable-based payment schedule. No salary split rules — it's a service fee. Price at 95-98% of published budget ceiling |

## 5e. BID/IDB Competitive Procurement — Comparación de Precios (CP)

CTH's first BID procurement bid was the ESPOL e-learning platform (EC-L1261-P00203, May 2026). This is a fundamentally different process from narrative-based grants. Key differences and lessons:

### How BID CP Works

BID Comparación de Precios is a **lowest evaluated price** competition — the cheapest technically compliant bid wins. There is no narrative scoring, no theory of change, no impact framework. The evaluation is binary: you either meet all technical requirements or you're disqualified, and then it's purely on price.

**Implications for CTH:**
- Don't invest time in persuasive narratives. Focus 100% on compliance with technical requirements and pricing.
- Fill in the SDC (Solicitud de Cotizaciones) forms exactly as specified. No creative formatting.
- The 40% salary rule does NOT apply — BID CP bids are service subscriptions, not personnel-heavy grant budgets.

### SDC Form Package (Standard BID CP Template)

```
FORMULARIOS DE LA OFERTA
=========================
1. Carta de la Oferta — formal offer letter with all required BID clauses
2. Formulario de Información sobre el Oferente — corporate info, NIT, legal rep
3. Formulario APCA — only if consortium (usually N/A for CTH solo bids)
4. Lista de Precios y Cronograma — deliverable-by-deliverable pricing
5. Personal Clave — CV summaries in the funder's required table format
6. Declaración de Mantenimiento de Oferta — bid validity commitment
7. Garantía de Cumplimiento — performance guarantee (check if NO APLICA)
8. Autorización del Fabricante — only for hardware (usually NO APLICA)
```

### Pricing Strategy for CP Bids

- Budget is published in the procurement announcement — bid 95-98% of the ceiling to be competitive while leaving margin.
- Break deliverables into the payment structure the client defines (e.g., initial config 10% + quarterly operations 15% each).
- Price in the currency specified (usually USD for BID).
- No IVA — international organizations are tax-exempt.

### Qualification Criteria (What Kills BID Bids)

BID CP has hard qualification gates. Miss any one and you're out:

1. **Years of experience** (e.g., "5 years SaaS experience") — CTH as an entity may not meet this. Mitigation: leverage team members' individual experience where the SDC allows it.
2. **Similar projects** (e.g., "3 similar contracts with actas/certificados") — Requires formal completion certificates. Start collecting these from team members WEEKS before the deadline, not days.
3. **Key staff** (e.g., "PM + Technical Lead with CVs") — Must match the profiles described in the technical specs exactly.

### Lesson from ESPOL (May 2026)

**Initial fit assessment was "Low" — revised to "Medium-low" after discovering team CVs and La Molina evidence.** The structural gap was that CTH as an entity lacks SaaS vendor track record, but individual team members (Angélica: 76K-user LMS for Gobernación de Cundinamarca; Giovanny: 10+ years Moodle across UNITEC, Uniempresarial, CUN, ESAP) have exactly the right experience. The La Molina PROINNÓVATE consultancy (Contract N°1028, Orden de Servicio 3227, S/38,000) proved CTH can deliver to a university client under government procurement.

**Key takeaway**: For tech service bids, always check if the team's combined experience closes the entity-level gap before ruling the opportunity out.

## 6. Deliverables — What You Promise to Produce

### 6a. Standard Deliverable Categories

Every CTH grant should produce deliverables in these categories. The specific items depend on the grant, but the categories are consistent:

**Programmatic deliverables:**
- Startup pipeline report (applications received, screened, selected)
- Bootcamp/acceleration curriculum and materials
- Mentoring logs and feedback summaries
- Demo day event report with investor participation data
- Portfolio company progress reports

**Knowledge products:**
- Ecosystem mapping or landscape study
- Best practices documentation
- Case studies of successful startups
- Lessons learned report

**Financial deliverables:**
- Financial status reports (quarterly or bi-annual per funder)
- Transaction lists with supporting documentation
- Cost share documentation and verification
- Final financial report with auditor's statement (if required)

**M&E deliverables:**
- Baseline assessment (beginning of grant)
- Progress reports against KPIs (quarterly/bi-annual)
- Mid-term review (for grants >18 months)
- Terminal evaluation or final impact report
- LVGA report (for UNDP grants specifically)

### 6b. Reporting Cadence

| Grant size | Typical reporting | CTH's track record |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| < EUR 50K | Final report only | CLP26 — simple completion report |
| EUR 50K–150K | Bi-annual progress + final | UNDP-AFCIA — bi-annual FSRs, LVGA final |
| > EUR 150K | Quarterly progress + mid-term + final | P4G — quarterly reports, transaction lists, cost share docs |

**CTH's reporting strength**: Maintained discipline across all grants. This is one of the reasons funders re-funded CTH (P4G R3→R4, UNDP-AFCIA R1→R2). Protect this reputation.

### 6c. Deliverable Scheduling Rule

Work backwards from the grant end date:
- **Final report**: Due 30-60 days after grant end (check funder terms)
- **Terminal evaluation**: Start 3 months before grant end
- **Last progress report**: Submit before final report to avoid overlap
- **All programmatic activities**: Complete 2 months before grant end to allow for reporting

Never schedule a demo day or major event in the final month of a grant — it creates reporting chaos.

## 8. Pipeline Management

### 8a. Decision Speed Matters

From CTH's experience, grant pipelines have very different timelines:

| Speed | Examples | Implication |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| **Fast** (< 3 months from submission to award) | CLP/Climate KIC, GIZ direct invitation | Can be reactive. Pursue when calls open |
| **Medium** (3–9 months) | P4G, UNDP, Expertise France | Need to plan ahead. Start consortium building before the call opens |
| **Slow** (> 12 months) | DGGF, NL Embassy bilateral, Adaptation Fund | Relationship-driven. Maintain contact regardless of active calls |

### 8b. Avoiding CTH's Known Failure Patterns

These patterns have specifically cost CTH grants or wasted capacity:

1. **The unresponsive partner trap**: Energy Catalyst R11 died because Eva Escamilla (Carbon Trust) never responded to 3 follow-ups. Rule: if a critical partner hasn't responded in 2 weeks, find an alternative. Don't wait.

2. **The salary-heavy budget**: P4G R5 rejected. Rule: every budget goes through the 40% check before Gideon sees it.

3. **The wrong-version attachment**: P4G R5 submitted with incorrect W&B file. Rule: use the pre-submission checklist (Section 4d). Every attachment verified by name and version.

4. **The capacity crunch**: Multiple emails reference CTH operating under "beperkte capaciteit." Rule: never have more than 2 active grant drafts in the same 3-week window. If a third opportunity appears, either defer it or bring in external grant-writing support.

5. **The impact measurement gap**: DGGF follow-up exposed that CTH wasn't measuring impact systematically. Rule: every new proposal either references existing impact data or includes a budget line for building the measurement framework.

6. **The entity-vs-team experience mismatch** (new, from ESPOL 2026): CTH as a legal entity is young (registered 2021) and may not meet "X years of company experience" requirements in competitive bids. But team members individually have 8-10+ years of relevant experience. When evaluating opportunities, always check: (a) does the bid allow individual experience to supplement entity experience? (b) can team CVs close the gap? The ESPOL bid was initially scored "Low fit" but moved to "Medium-low" after discovering the team's LMS credentials and the La Molina contract.

7. **The late document hunt**: For ESPOL, experience certificates from team members' prior employers were identified as the #1 bottleneck with 3 days to deadline. Rule: the moment a competitive bid moves to GO, immediately ask all named team members to locate and upload their project certificates/contracts. Don't wait for the forms to be drafted first.

### 9b. Drive Folder Organization for Grants

All grant/bid files go in the CTH Drive structure: `CLEANTECHHUB → Proposals → [YEAR] → [OPPORTUNITY-ID]`. When starting a new bid:
1. Create the year folder if it doesn't exist (e.g., `2026`)
2. Create a subfolder named by the opportunity ID (e.g., `ESPOL-EC-L1261-P00203`)
3. Number files with a prefix to keep them ordered: `01_Oferta...`, `02_Certificado...`, etc.
4. Include a subfolder for originals (e.g., `04_SDC_Originales`) to store the procurement docs as received
5. Copy (don't move) supporting docs from their original Drive locations — never move the originals
