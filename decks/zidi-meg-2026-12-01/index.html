Participant Q3

This model earns from usage, not from permanent grants

  • Scalable business: comodato model leases smart chargers; recurring kWh-based revenue via MubOn Cloud (IoT metering, billing, CO₂ reporting).
  • Subsidy-dependent: operations that stop when the grant ends, with no customer revenue trail.
  • Public page: 30% cost-share requirement fulfilled — grant was catalytic, with counterpart funding.
  • My test for any SME: does revenue continue if the grant ends?

Zidi thesis checklist · applied to MubOn

Here is the readiness test I use before I take a climate SME to investors

  • Market trend: national EV growth and charging interoperability policy (PNIC target 3,000+ new stations by end 2026).
  • Sustainability of the company: kWh revenue, cost-share met, grant catalytic not permanent.
  • Growth metrics: sites, kW, sessions, users, revenue reconciled in one public narrative.
  • Externalities: emissions avoided, jobs, gender mix reported; negative externalities not itemised on the public page.
  • Mid- and long-term ambition: reach investment-ready scale within the grant window (no disclosed target beyond that).

Participant Q1

I weigh regulated platforms against direct angel or equity tickets

  • Platforms (regulated crowdfunding or investment platforms): lower minimum ticket, structured onboarding, and diligence run for you — but fees, standardised terms, and limited say on governance.
  • Direct (angel or private equity): you choose the cheque size, shape minority rights, and stay close to the founder — but you carry more diligence, FX, and repatriation work yourself.
  • I compare minimum ticket, who does diligence, board or veto rights, how capital returns in euros, and credible exit paths before I sign.
  • MubOn’s path mixed both: many conversations, then a reported angel round after the venture could show usage-based revenue — illustrative, not a template for your ticket size.

Source: https://data.cleantechhub.net/projects/mubon/ (angel round); qualitative guidance — not legal advice

Participant Q4

How I compare risk–return across agriculture, fintech, and renewables

Agriculture

  • Revenue visibility: seasonal; depends on harvest and buyer contracts.
  • Capex: land, inputs, cold chain — often lumpy.
  • Weather & seasonality: high operational risk.
  • Regulation & FX: export rules, phytosanitary permits, commodity FX.
  • Time to exit: longer; trade-sale or regional buyer more common than quick IPO.

Fintech / climate data

  • Revenue visibility: recurring if churn is controlled; proof needs live users.
  • Capex: mostly people and software; lighter asset base.
  • Weather & seasonality: low direct exposure; demand cyclicality still matters.
  • Regulation & FX: licensing, data privacy, partner-bank rules; FX on cross-border flows.
  • Time to exit: can be faster if metrics scale — but regulatory drag can slow M&A.

Renewables (incl. EV charging)

  • Revenue visibility: tariff or kWh usage; grids and policy shape demand.
  • Capex: hardware-heavy upfront; financing structure drives risk.
  • Weather & seasonality: solar yield varies; charging follows mobility patterns.
  • Regulation & FX: grid codes, permits, tariff resets; equipment often dollar-linked.
  • Time to exit: infrastructure buyers or pools once assets are metered and contracted.

Qualitative comparison only — no return forecasts; EV example: https://data.cleantechhub.net/projects/mubon/

Participant Q5 & Q6

Political risk diligence — and what diaspora capital does differently

My regulatory checklist (Q5)

  • Sector rules & permits: licences that bind the asset — MubOn mapped charging rules in Bogotá and Cali.
  • Tariffs & price regulation: what can be charged, indexed, or re-set by government.
  • Election & policy cycle: which incentives survive a handover.
  • FX & capital controls: how dividends or repayments cross borders.
  • Contracts & legal structure: enforceability, minority protections, and fiscal host if grants were involved.

Diaspora vs institutions (Q6)

I see diaspora investors move earlier — smaller tickets, founder trust, and patience while the model proves itself. Development banks and commercial lenders usually need audited MRV, a fiscal host trail, and a data room. Neither replaces the other; they enter at different readiness thresholds.
