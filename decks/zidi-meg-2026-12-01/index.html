Participant Q3

This model earns from usage, not from permanent grants

  • Scalable business: comodato model leases smart chargers; recurring kWh-based revenue via MubOn Cloud (IoT metering, billing, CO₂ reporting).
  • Subsidy-dependent: operations that stop when the grant ends, with no customer revenue trail.
  • Public page: 30% cost-share requirement fulfilled — grant was catalytic, with counterpart funding.
  • My test for any SME: does revenue continue if the grant ends?

Zidi thesis checklist · applied to MubOn

Here is the readiness test I use before I take a climate SME to investors

  • Market trend: national EV growth and charging interoperability policy (PNIC target 3,000+ new stations by end 2026).
  • Sustainability of the company: kWh revenue, cost-share met, grant catalytic not permanent.
  • Growth metrics: sites, kW, sessions, users, revenue reconciled in one public narrative.
  • Externalities: emissions avoided, jobs, gender mix reported; negative externalities not itemised on the public page.
  • Mid- and long-term ambition: reach investment-ready scale within the grant window (no disclosed target beyond that).

Participant questions 1–3

How to invest, which sectors, and how to spot a real business

Platforms vs direct (Q1)

Both exist in LatAm. Here, early capital arrived as a USD 20,000 angel cheque after 15 investor engagements — not legal advice; diligence the instrument and governance.

Profitable impact SMEs (Q2)

EV charging shows measured kWh, sessions, and avoided emissions in a growing market. I will not invent profitability figures for agriculture or fintech in this deck.

Scalable vs subsidy (Q3)

See comodato + MubOn Cloud: revenue tied to usage. Ask whether customer revenue survives after the grant.

Participant questions 4–6

Risk-return, rules, and what diaspora can do differently

Risk-return (Q4)

Only return figure quoted here: 24.3% IRR on Pool #1. Cross-sector comparisons (agri, fintech, renewables) need their own models — not stated in this case.

Political & regulatory risk (Q5)

Worked in public: regulatory mapping in Bogotá and Cali, six workshops, national interoperability mandate. Diligence the rules that bind the asset.

Diaspora vs institutions (Q6)

Diaspora can often move earlier, with smaller tickets and closer founder trust. Institutions usually arrive when MRV, fiscal host, and a data room exist.
