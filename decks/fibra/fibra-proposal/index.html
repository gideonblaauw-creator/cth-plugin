Four Structural Drivers for a Fall 2026 Launch

🇪🇺 EU Compliance Pressure (CSRD + Digital Product Passport)

EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) and the incoming Digital Product Passport (2027) create a legally-backed compliance market for LatAm supply chain startups. Ventures that are investor-ready before the mandate are first movers.

📦 Nearshoring Tailwinds

Post-pandemic supply-chain reshoring is redirecting textile and apparel sourcing from Asia to the Americas. USMCA + Central American manufacturing creates structural demand for FIBRA Track 2 (textile innovation) and Track 3 (supply chain transparency) ventures.

🛍 Gen Z Conscious Consumption

73% of Gen Z LatAm consumers prioritise sustainability credentials. Brands sourcing from FIBRA ventures gain a certified, impact-documented supply chain — converting ESG reporting into a commercial advantage.

♻️ Circular Economy Policy Momentum

Colombia, Peru, and Costa Rica have passed extended producer responsibility legislation targeting textiles. FIBRA ventures are positioned as the implementation layer for government circular economy commitments.

Section 03

The White Space — Seven Critical Gaps

Twelve existing programmes were mapped across Latin America. None combines all four of FIBRA's core pillars simultaneously. FIBRA is not competing — it is creating a category.

Gap 1 · No Pan-Regional Impact Cohort

10/10

Zero programmes operate across all five REIN countries with a combined fashion + climate + gender mandate. Every existing programme is either national or single-thematic.

Gap 2 · EU Compliance Pipeline Missing

9.5/10

No LatAm accelerator specifically prepares ventures for CSRD reporting or Digital Product Passport compliance. The 2027 deadline creates a time-bound first-mover window.

Gap 3 · Corporate Innovation Untapped

9/10

Regional corporates (Falabella, Liverpool, Grupo Éxito) have sustainability commitments but no structured startup sourcing pipeline. FIBRA is the supply side of that equation.

Gap 4 · Investment Readiness Absent

9/10

11 active impact investors were identified with appetite for FIBRA-type deals. None has a dedicated LatAm fashion programme to source from. FIBRA closes the deal-flow gap.

Gap 5 · Gender + Climate Mandates Separated

8.5/10

Kerry Bannigan is the only leader in LatAm fashion who has built a fund explicitly combining gender equity and climate mandates. No accelerator has integrated both. FIBRA's SDG 5 + SDG 13 dual mandate — channelled through PVBLIC — is a first.

Gap 6 · Artisan-to-Startup Bridge Missing

8.5/10

3.2 million artisan craftspeople (UNESCO) operate without any startup acceleration pathway. FIBRA Track 3 is the only structured programme converting artisan heritage into investable ventures.

FIBRA is not the best programme in a crowded field. It is the first programme in a field that does not yet exist — the pan-LatAm, impact-first, investment-ready fashion accelerator.

Section 05

Pilot Activations — Sourcing, Curriculum & Sprint

Three complementary mechanisms that launch FIBRA with momentum: a trusted internal pipeline, a public open call, and a pre-cohort activation event that builds ecosystem energy before the first cohort begins.

🔍 Track 1 · Internal Database Sourcing

The first sourcing step is a targeted review of the CleantechHUB and REIN startup databases across all fashion sub-sectors: sustainable materials, circular economy, supply chain transparency, fashion tech, artisan heritage, and gender-inclusive workforce. Shortlisted ventures from the database receive priority invitations — reinforcing trust within our existing community and ensuring a strong baseline for Cohort 1.

Strategic rationale: our databases are an underutilised asset. Starting internally strengthens cohort quality from day one and demonstrates REIN's network depth to PVBLIC and future funders.

📣 Track 2 · Open Call via REIN Hub Network

In parallel, FIBRA launches a structured public open call targeting entrepreneurs across all fashion sub-sectors — materials, circular economy, supply chain, consumer products, technology, artisan crafts, and more. In all five REIN hub countries (Colombia, Peru, Costa Rica, Guatemala, El Salvador), the call is promoted actively through local hub channels, events, and partner networks.

Key outcome: the open call is not just recruitment — it is market education. It signals publicly that a dedicated programme for fashion impact entrepreneurship has arrived in Latin America, and begins building the broader practitioner community that will fuel 2027+.

Curriculum Design Principles

FIBRA is a niche-focus programme for the fashion industry — not a generic accelerator. While business development is a core component, the primary goal is to bring together entrepreneurs from all fashion sectors under one structured framework aligned around impact, sustainability, and cross-sector collaboration. The curriculum is co-designed with PVBLIC.

①

Business-Focused, Not Purely Commercial

Founders develop strategy, impact metrics, and investor readiness alongside their market thesis — not instead of it.

②

Industry-Specific

Sessions, mentors, and challenges curated for the fashion ecosystem. No generic startup content — every module is fashion-contextualized.

③

Cross-Sector Cohort

Textile innovators, supply chain startups, artisan ventures, and circular economy players learn together and from each other.

④

Community as Output

The cohort becomes an ongoing network — not just a group of graduates. Alumni connection is a FIBRA deliverable, not a by-product.

The 2026 pilot cohort is explicitly designed as a learning and proof-of-concept phase — built with intentional flexibility to evolve into a more comprehensive programme in 2027 with expanded cohort size, deeper curriculum, and stronger funder and corporate partner commitments.
