What is MEL Builder?

MEL Builder is a free tool that helps nonprofit practitioners find the right approach to Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning (MEL) for their organisation — and get a practical design guide to implement it.

Most nonprofits struggle with MEL not because they lack commitment, but because the approach they're trying to use doesn't fit their capacity, context, or purpose. MEL Builder asks two focused steps of questions, suggests 2–3 well-matched MEL approaches, and then builds a step-by-step design guide for the one you choose.

It's a starting point — not a substitute for expert MEL support. But it's a structured, practical way to get unstuck.

How it works

1

Two steps of questions

Your MEL situation, purpose, and capacity — all clickable cards. Then a short open text field to describe your organisation, beneficiaries, existing data, and what you have in mind for reporting.

2

AI suggests 2–3 approaches

Matched to your specific context. Each card explains why the approach fits your situation, how long it takes to set up, and what it helps you understand.

3

You pick one & refine

Select the approach that fits best. Answer two quick questions — who collects data, and your total reach — plus an optional notes field for constraints or funder requirements.

4

You get a design guide + real examples

A step-by-step MEL design guide: what to measure, data tools, review rhythm, roles, and a setup timeline. Plus an optional button to search for real published examples of the approach used by NGOs — sourced live from the web.

Advocacy, policy change & systems change ✦ Featured
Laudes Foundation Rubrics
A rubrics-based measurement framework developed by Laudes Foundation for organisations working on systems change — especially policy reform, climate action, financial sector change, and long-horizon advocacy work where outcomes are slow to materialise and impossible to count with simple metrics.

The system uses 21 rubrics across four levels: Category A assesses process quality (design, implementation, monitoring, communication, capacity); Category B tracks early and intermediate systems change conditions (stakeholder-informed policies, investor pressure, multi-stakeholder movements, compelling narratives, worker voice, and more); Category C tracks medium-term outcomes (policymakers reforming, financial actors shifting, businesses transforming); Category D tracks long-term impact (bold policy frameworks, accountable finance, responsible industries).

Each rubric is rated on a five-point scale: Harmful → Unconducive → Partly Conducive → Conducive & Supportive → Thrivable. Organisations select 1–4 relevant rubrics from B and C that match their initiative, always add A5 (organisational capacity), establish a baseline, and re-assess at regular intervals — using mixed evidence from multiple sources. The rating describes the state of the system the organisation is working to change, not just the organisation's own outputs.

The rubrics are purposefully generic so they work across sectors, geographies, and scales — and are available for free use by any organisation involved in systems change.
